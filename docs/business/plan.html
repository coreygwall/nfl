Everyone else is running toward gambling.

Splash Sports has raised $28.6M and just partnered with Polymarket. Sleeper is at a $400M valuation with roughly 60% of revenue from real-money picks. That leaves the largest part of the market unserved: the ordinary group pool played for twenty dollars and bragging rights — the one that includes somebody's kid and somebody's father-in-law who will not be creating an account. Tally already owns the hardest asset for serving it: joining is typing your name. The business is to become the default way a group keeps score across every season of the year, and to charge the one person with the pain — the commissioner.

  • ~54M Americans in pools yearly
  • $0.00013 / player / month to run
  • ~98% gross margin at every scale

01The short version

Nine conclusions

If you read nothing else, read these. Each links to the document that argues it.

  1. Don't touch prize money — ever.

    It triggers money-transmitter licensing in up to 50 states and Apple Guideline 5.3, which forces real-money apps to be free on the App Store and bars IAP for entry credit. That single rule would kill the subscription business inside the iOS app. Build a kitty tracker that hands off to Venmo instead — it's more useful for a 15-person pool and carries none of the liability.

    02 · Monetization

  2. Charge the commissioner. Never the players, never per-player.

    Per-player pricing makes the commissioner do arithmetic about the eleventh invite — the one behaviour that must never happen. Every pricing page in this category gets it wrong.

    02 · Monetization

  3. Don't wait for one big spring launch — ship continuously.

    The bulk NFL pool-formation window closes in September, but that's an argument against a generic re-launch push, not against building or marketing. An NFL playoffs pool ships before Wild Card weekend, and a non-traditional March Madness product — not another bracket clone — ships with real runway before Selection Sunday, both fast builds on the existing architecture.

    05 · Go-to-market

  4. Cost is not a variable in this business.

    Gross margin holds at ~98% from 20 players to 3 million. Stop optimising infrastructure — there is nothing there. Spend every available hour on distribution.

    03 · Unit economics

  5. The Weekly Recap is the highest-value unbuilt feature.

    An AI-written wrap of the group's week, by name. It costs about $0.002 per pool per week, it is the most natural paywall in the product, and it is distribution — it's the thing a commissioner forwards into the group chat with a link attached.

    06 · Engagement

  6. Side games run on standing, not money.

    Being named in front of people you know, loss-aversion on a streak, and social debt. Not one of the mechanics worth building needs a prize.

    06 · Engagement

  7. Don't raise.

    There is nothing to spend it on at $60/year of infrastructure, the metric that would justify it takes twelve months to measure, and venture money will push the product toward exactly the gambling model this whole plan avoids. Bootstrapping to ~$196k/year at 98% margin is the better founder outcome.

    07 · Partnerships & investment

  8. Measure one number above all others.

    What percentage of commissioners run a second season. Below ~35% this is a novelty; above 50% it's a business. Nothing else is decidable until it's known — and it takes a full year to find out, so start now.

    05 · Go-to-market

  9. There are three kinds of competition here, not one.

    A pool is you predicting something a feed decides. A card is you playing something the group decides — golf, poker. A call is you predicting anything at all, resolved by the group. The roster of people is what makes them one product rather than three, and calls are the only thing on any roadmap that works in July.

02Strategy · doc 01

The market, and the gap in it

The addressable universe is roughly 54 million American pool participants a year. At 15 people per pool that's about 3.6 million pools. Splash's 2M users represent maybe 130,000 of them. The incumbents have captured single-digit percentages. The real competitor isn't Splash — it's Excel.

Why the gap is defensible

A company whose business model is real-money gaming cannot be the product you put your 11-year-old on, or text to your mother-in-law, or run in a company Slack. Splash and Sleeper are structurally barred from following Tally here, because doing so means building a product that cannibalizes their own revenue.

Frictionless identity

Name → in, with a passkey offered on the way past and device codes for a second phone. Competitors lose 40–70% of invited players at account creation. Tally loses almost none. Already shipped.

Managed entries

One account runs up to 12 entries — kids, a partner, the friend who won't install anything. No competitor does this, because their model needs every entry to be a monetizable account. Already shipped.

Cost structure

One Cloudflare Worker, one D1 database. At 1.5M players the bill is about $1,500/year. It's what makes a genuinely good free tier permanently affordable.

03Monetization · doc 02

The freemium ladder

The paywall's job is to be invisible to a first-time commissioner and obvious to a returning one. Nothing that makes a pool work is ever paid. What's paid is everything that makes a pool persist, multiply, and be fun to run.

Free

$0 forever
  • One pool, up to 20 players
  • The full game and both boards
  • Face ID, device codes, managed entries
  • iOS app, push, Live Activities
  • Automatic results and schedule sync

The free tier is the marketing budget: every free pool is 15 people being shown the product by someone they trust.

Tally Plus

$29 / season · $49 / yr
  • Up to 200 players — the 21st person is the trigger
  • The Weekly Recap
  • Multiple simultaneous pools
  • Season archive & history
  • Side games — props, dares, rivalries
  • The kitty tracker + split-the-ante
  • Custom pool branding

Clubhouse

$99 / year, per group
  • Everything, across unlimited pools
  • A group home at /g/<slug>
  • Cross-season standings — Group of the Year
  • Co-commissioners
  • All-time record book

The retention product. A group four years in has a trophy case it cannot export.

The move worth making

Split the ante. Most pools already collect money. A "the group covers it" flow — the commissioner taps split with the pool, everyone sees "$2.50 each" — reframes a $29 ask as pocket change. It rides free on the kitty tracker and matches how the money already moves.

The one cost that scales

The Weekly Recap is the only component whose cost grows with usage. At Haiku 4.5 rates a recap runs $0.0065 — dropping to about $0.002 with the batch API and prompt caching, since the voice and format prompt is identical across every pool.

05Engagement · doc 06

Games on top, and why anyone plays them

Tally can't offer prizes, and shouldn't want to. That forces the honest question: why do people keep playing a pool they're losing? Not for the $200 — because twelve people they know will see the result.

The design rule

A side game is worth building if it produces something the group will talk about. If it only produces a number, it's a leaderboard, and nobody needs another leaderboard.

What the Weekly Recap sounds like

Dana won the week with 13, which she will mention. The 5-pointer on Detroit was the only one anybody had, and she had it while the rest of the group took Kansas City for the fourth week running.

Corey is now 0–3 on his own 5-point picks and has not moved off the Bears. This is week four.

Everyone survived. Nobody is more than 11 back. Thursday is Green Bay at Chicago, and one of you should probably take Green Bay.

A competitor can copy the feature in a week. They cannot copy four years of your group's history feeding the prompt.

06Go-to-market · doc 05

The calendar decides everything

One commissioner brings fifteen players. Players cost nothing to acquire. Commissioners are the entire funnel — so every hour of GTM answers one of two questions: how does a commissioner find Tally, and how does a player become one?

  1. Sep – Nov 2026

    Market & build, concurrently

    Ship iOS, the Recap, the CSV importer — and market the weekly pool the whole time. Playoffs-pool build starts in November, not after a quiet stretch.

  2. Dec 2026 · real date

    Playoffs pool live

    Before Wild Card weekend. Lower-commitment format, built fast on the existing pool-type architecture — the first proof that new formats ship in weeks.

  3. January 2027

    Spreadsheet Amnesty

    End-of-season artifacts while the Excel pain is fresh. The non-traditional March product gets scoped and built.

  4. Feb – Mar 2027 · real date

    March product live

    Real runway before Selection Sunday because the build started in January. Not another bracket clone — the market already has plenty of those.

  5. Aug 2027

    The season

    The biggest single push, prepared for by continuous marketing and two off-season products' worth of proof — not by a five-month quiet period.

Channels worth the hours

  1. Your own network, instrumented. No strategy substitutes for watching a commissioner who isn't you get stuck.
  2. "Steal the spreadsheet." A CSV importer that targets the exact switching moment. A day or two of work; it's a feature, a landing page and a Reddit post at once.
  3. Reddit, earned. Answer the "best free pick'em site" threads honestly. Agents draft; humans post.
  4. The App Store. SwiftUI on iOS 26 with Liquid Glass, passkeys and Live Activities is exactly what Apple features. Worth more than any paid channel available.
  5. SEO, agent-written. Pages published in October rank in March. Target 30–50 before February.

Channels to skip

Paid social, paid search, and influencer deals in any sports-adjacent keyword are priced by sportsbooks with ~$300 LTVs. You cannot compete at $49 ARPU and shouldn't try.

Sportsbook affiliate revenue is the easiest money in this category and the fastest way to become the thing Tally exists in opposition to. Decline it in advance, so it isn't decided later under revenue pressure.

Why not to raise

  • Nothing to spend it on. Infrastructure is ~$60/year today. The constraint is founder attention, which capital doesn't buy back for two people with day jobs.
  • The key metric doesn't exist yet. Commissioner retention takes twelve months to measure. Raising first means raising on a story.
  • Venture math doesn't fit. $100M revenue at $49 ARPU means 2M paying commissioners — about 25M pools, seven times the entire estimated US market.
  • Capital pushes toward gambling. A board looking at Sleeper's revenue mix asks the obvious question every quarter.

The likeliest good outcome

An acquisition — most plausibly by Splash, who are consolidating this exact category and cannot build a free family tier themselves. What they'd be buying, in order:

  1. The identity system — droppable into their own funnel on day one
  2. Commissioner retention data
  3. The non-gambling brand, worth more as the category gets politically expensive
  4. A 98% gross margin cost structure

Which argues for the same things bootstrapping does: don't take sportsbook money, don't build a real-money feature, don't sign an exclusive.

08Brand · doc 04

Where your group keeps score

Before there were apps there was a sheet of paper on the break-room wall. Somebody drew the grid, somebody wrote the names down the side, and every Monday one person updated it in pen. That person is the reason any of it happened — the keeper of the tally.

The enemy

Somebody decided the best thing about the pool — that everybody's in it — was a customer acquisition cost. So they put an account form in front of it, then a deposit screen, then live odds down the side. The pool still exists. It just isn't for your kid anymore, or your mother-in-law, or the guy in accounts who doesn't have the app. We think the pool was fine.

The voice already exists

The product's own copy is the strongest brand asset Tally has — dry, plain-spoken, generous, never hyped:

"Showing up late is okay — pick from the games that are left."

"A winner every week. One bad Sunday is never fatal."

"Fill it in, watch it burn by the second round."

Words Tally doesn't use

bet · wager · odds · action · degen · sweat · juice · cash out · deposit · parlay · book

A hard list. The premise is that Tally isn't that thing, and one stray "lock of the week" undoes a lot of careful work. Protect the visual system for the same reason — the distance from a sportsbook is the positioning.

"Your friends join by typing their name."

Demonstrably true, technically hard, and the thing no competitor can say.

"You can enter picks for your kids."

Instantly legible, emotionally specific, zero competitors.

"There's no money in it, and that's the feature."

Reframes the missing sportsbook as the reason to choose it.
